Programmatic Advertising in 2025: What Product Leaders Need to Know

Written by Ranjan Dasgupta · Programmatic · ~6 min read

Programmatic advertising in 2025 looks very different from the first wave of RTB. Identity has fragmented, privacy has become a hard constraint, and buyers are far more deliberate about supply path optimization. Yet for product leaders, the core responsibility remains the same: design exchanges and monetization products that are resilient to change and honest about trade-offs.

From my perspective as an AdTech product leader, the most important shift is that exchanges are no longer just pipes for bids. They are opinionated products. Decisions about which signals to include, how to structure auctions, and how to treat deals versus open auction all encode a point of view about the marketplace. Product leaders who ignore this will ship “flexible” APIs that no one truly understands.

Another reality in 2025 is that programmatic cannot be separated from regulation and platform changes. Browser privacy features, mobile platform policies, and regional regulations all shape what data is available at bid time. Rather than trying to fight this, exchanges need to lean into contextual intelligence, better creative relevance, and tighter feedback loops with buyers.

For organizations building or reshaping their exchanges, I recommend starting with three questions: What is the cleanest representation of your inventory? How do you want buyers to experience your marketplace? And how will you measure whether those experiences are improving? The answers will shape your programmatic advertising strategy for years to come.

Finally, programmatic in 2025 is more multi-surface than ever. Web, App, and CTV monetization are converging in buyers’ workflows, even if they are still separate in your internal teams. Building shared concepts and shared infrastructure across screens is one of the highest-leverage moves a product leader can make.

CTV Monetization: Building Revenue at the Convergence of TV and Digital

Written by Ranjan Dasgupta · CTV · ~7 min read

CTV monetization sits at the intersection of television economics and digital ad technology. When I speak with teams building CTV products, the most common misconception I encounter is that you can simply “port” Web display or in-stream strategies into TV. In practice, CTV requires a deeper understanding of content, viewer expectations, and the constraints of large-screen experiences.

The technical foundation starts with SSAI and CSAI. SSAI gives you the reliability and UX you need for big screens, while CSAI can enable richer interactivity. Rather than picking one approach dogmatically, I encourage teams to weigh their content types, partner ecosystem, and measurement needs before making choices. Whatever you choose, log-level visibility and strong observability are non-negotiable.

From there, the big strategic question is how CTV fits into your broader programmatic advertising and direct sales motion. Buyers increasingly expect CTV to be available through the same platforms and deals they use for other digital video. That means your exchange representation, packaging, and deal workflows must feel familiar while still honoring the unique aspects of TV-like consumption.

Measurement remains a moving target. Panels, ACR data, and log-level signals each have their own strengths and limitations. As a product leader, you do not need to solve all of measurement, but you do need a clear story for buyers: what you can measure today, what you are improving, and how that aligns with their CTV objectives.

Ultimately, the CTV products I’ve seen succeed are the ones that respect the viewer and the buyer equally. They avoid overloading pods, provide clean and trustworthy supply paths, and integrate seamlessly with omnichannel planning. That is the bar I use when thinking about any new CTV monetization initiative.

Header Bidding Optimization: Beyond the Basics

Written by Ranjan Dasgupta · Header Bidding · ~5 min read

Header bidding is no longer a new idea, but many stacks still look like archaeology sites: layers of historical decisions, half-remembered experiments, and adapters that no one wants to touch. When I help teams with header bidding and Prebid optimization, the first step is almost always simplification.

Simplification does not mean making the stack less powerful. It means focusing on the bidders that consistently deliver value, aligning timeout settings with real network conditions, and instrumenting the system so you can see what is happening. In my experience, good monitoring and logging deliver more yield uplift than exotic bidding tricks.

Another underappreciated dimension is how header bidding interacts with your yield optimization strategy. If your floors, deals, and exchange behavior are misaligned with header bidding logic, you’ll see weird pricing artifacts and frustrated buyers. Treating header bidding as part of a holistic auction strategy—rather than a separate “pre” step—helps resolve these frictions.

Finally, be honest about client-side versus server-side trade-offs. Client-side gives you transparency and fine-grained control, but at the cost of performance and implementation complexity. Server-side reduces page weight and improves scale, but obscures parts of the auction. Many of the best setups I see today use both, with clear rules about which partners belong where.

Supply Path Optimization: Why SPO Matters More Than Ever

Written by Ranjan Dasgupta · SPO · ~5 min read

When I talk to buyers, their view of the open web often starts with one word: noisy. Hundreds of supply paths, many of them duplicative, all promising access to the same impressions. Supply path optimization emerged as their answer to this noise, and it has quickly become a default expectation rather than a niche initiative.

For publishers and exchanges, this means that every path must earn its keep. If a reseller chain cannot explain the incremental value it brings—unique demand, better tools, or specialized access—it is at risk of being deprioritized. This is where clean ads.txt/app-ads.txt and sellers.json files become more than compliance checkboxes; they are your supply narrative.

In my own work on SPO strategy, I focus on three questions: Which paths are truly unique? Which paths are adding hidden cost or latency? And which paths do buyers explicitly prefer? Log-level data, partner feedback, and experimentation all feed into those answers.

Good SPO work is not anti-intermediary; it is pro-clarity. When buyers understand why a certain path exists and see performance to match, they are happy to lean into it. When they do not, or when multiple indistinguishable paths compete, the default outcome is consolidation—often away from publishers and partners who are not telling a clear story.

Agentic AI and the Future of Advertising

Written by Ranjan Dasgupta · AI & Advertising · ~6 min read

Agentic AI—systems that can observe, decide, and act—will change how we operate monetization stacks just as profoundly as RTB did a decade ago. The question for product leaders is not whether AI will be used, but where it should be trusted first.

In my view, the earliest wins will come from decision spaces that are high-volume, repetitive, and measurable: floor adjustments, creative combinations, and anomaly detection across exchanges. These are areas where AI can act as a co-pilot to human operators, surfacing recommendations and automating low-risk actions.

Through AI in advertising work and the AdsGupta platform, I’ve been exploring how agentic systems can help teams reason about complex choices like SPO and yield trade-offs. The most promising approaches do not hide decisions; they expose them in more digestible ways, with clear levers and guardrails.

Looking ahead, I expect to see “monetization agents” that can propose and run controlled experiments, synthesize results, and recommend changes to GTM and product teams. With $200M+ in scaled revenue experience behind me, my caution is simple: make sure humans stay in the loop for high-impact calls, and design AI workflows that can explain themselves.

The future of advertising will not be purely algorithmic, but the teams that pair strong human judgment with well-designed AI systems will shape that future more than anyone else.
